# A real Nasdaq ITCH day: 2019-12-30

The first run of lobkit on real exchange data. Input: `12302019.NASDAQ_ITCH50.gz` from Nasdaq's public server (emi.nasdaq.com, directory ITCH/Nasdaq ITCH), 3,524,013,057 bytes, SHA-256 `ef03df46a27e6bda4dead017f84c2e3979df7211f02c7868b51d53fceb99c689`. The file is not committed. Everything below is reproduced by

    scripts/verify_itch_day.sh 12302019.NASDAQ_ITCH50.gz AAPL,MSFT,AMZN,TSLA,QQQ
    python3 scripts/itch_queue_front.py itch_work/day.bin AAPL MSFT AMZN TSLA QQQ

## 2. Five books against an independent reconstruction

`scripts/itch_reference_top.py` rebuilds the book of one stock with Python's struct module and dictionaries, without any lobkit code, and logs the best bid and ask with their displayed sizes after every change. `lobkit_replay --top-csv` logs the same from the engine. After collapsing repeated rows, the two sequences of (timestamp, bid, bid size, ask, ask size) are identical for all five stocks:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| MSFT | 1,220,969 | 576,026 | 35,315 | 54,873 | 600,038 | yes | 0 |
| AMZN | 535,575 | 232,975 | 15,123 | 54,500 | 67,604 | yes | 0 |

The book was never crossed (crossed_feed = 0 for every stock). Both reconstructions start from an empty book and end the day with no resting orders, so a daily ITCH file needs no seeding: the strict policy is correct for ITCH. LOBSTER files can still start mid-session; that question stays open (MARKET_DATA.md section 5).

## 3. What real data changed

**The automatic price band was wrong for every stock.** Every one of the five stocks has adds at 0.0001 dollars and others between 199,999 and 199,999.99 dollars on this day (far away orders that never trade). The tool centred its 65,536 tick band on the midpoint of that range, about 99,700 dollars, so every real order was rejected as out of band. When the range does not fit, the band is now centred on the median add price. What remains out of band are orders more than 327 dollars (half the band) from the median: AAPL 28, MSFT 9, AMZN 6,001 (2.6% of its adds, since a 655 dollar band is narrow for a 1,850 dollar stock), TSLA 624, QQQ 11. Sub-penny orders below one dollar are off the one cent tick and also rejected (AAPL 13, MSFT 12, QQQ 5). Later cancels, deletes and replaces of these orders are rejected as unknown ids and counted (AAPL 41, MSFT 21, AMZN 6,130, TSLA 628, QQQ 16). None of these orders was ever the best bid or ask in the reference reconstruction, which keeps them, so the identical top of book is not an artifact of dropping them.

**Executions are not always at the front of the queue.** lobkit counts a queue_order anomaly whenever an execution hits an order that is not first at its price, and the documentation said this means the replica has diverged from the feed. On real data the top of book matches an independent reconstruction exactly, yet the anomaly fires:

| Stock | E executions in continuous trading not at the front | C executions in continuous trading not at the front | Anomalies counted by lobkit |
|---|---|---|---|
| AAPL | 651 of 59,688 (1.1%) | 108 of 315 | 759 |
| MSFT | 1,075 of 34,336 (3.1%) | 514 of 565 | 1,589 |
| AMZN | 402 of 14,681 (2.7%) | 0 of 25 | 402 |
| TSLA | 572 of 36,472 (1.6%) | 4 of 104 | 576 |
| QQQ | 154 of 20,370 (0.8%) | 953 of 983 | 1,109 |

`scripts/itch_queue_front.py`, written separately, gives the same totals as the engine for every stock. Every execution in the opening and closing crosses and outside regular hours was at the front of its queue; the exceptions are all in continuous trading. All the C executions here are non-printable. The ITCH messages do not say why an order behind others was executed first, so this run does not explain it; it shows that strict price-time priority over displayed orders, which the simulator's queue position model assumes, does not hold for about 1 to 3 percent of displayed executions on this day. The anomaly therefore measures departures from strict FIFO in the feed, not divergence of the book.
